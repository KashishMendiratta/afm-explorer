# AFM force-curve artifacts and review cues

## Baseline drift and noise

A sloped or drifting pre-contact baseline can make a contact-point method choose a window too early. High-frequency noise, vibration and electrical interference can reduce fit stability. Review the raw curve and nearby pixels before assigning a material interpretation to an isolated extreme.

## Adhesion on retract

An adhesive interaction can keep the probe attached during retraction and produce a negative-force dip followed by a sudden snap-off. This feature is expected on the retract branch and should not be confused with the approach contact point.

## Saturation, multiple transitions and contamination

Clipped force values indicate detector or acquisition saturation. Multiple slope transitions can arise from layered samples, indentation, probe contamination or an unstable contact. These cases need manual review; a single linear slope may be an incomplete summary.

## Spatial checks

A single unusually stiff pixel surrounded by ordinary pixels is more suspicious than a coherent region with similar curves. Compare neighboring locations, both series, fit quality and the raw force-distance data. Treat explanations as hypotheses unless the acquisition and sample context support them.
